The Monty Hall Problem

3. Explore the mathematics

At the beginning, the probability that your chosen door hides the prize is 1/3. The probability that the prize is behind one of the two other doors is 2/3.

Because the host knows where the prize is and deliberately opens a goat door, that original 2/3 probability is concentrated on the one remaining unopened door. Therefore, switching wins with probability 2/3, while staying wins with probability 1/3.

Show a complete three-case explanation

Imagine that you always choose Door 1. There are three equally likely positions for the prize:

  1. Prize behind Door 1: staying wins; switching loses.
  2. Prize behind Door 2: staying loses; switching wins.
  3. Prize behind Door 3: staying loses; switching wins.

Switching wins in two of the three cases. The host's choice of which goat door to open does not change that conclusion.

A mathematical challenge

Suppose there are 100 doors, only one containing a prize. You choose one door. The host, knowing where the prize is, opens 98 other doors, all revealing goats. Should you stay or switch to the one other unopened door?

Show Explanation

Your original choice has a 1/100 chance of winning. The prize has a 99/100 chance of being among the other 99 doors. Once the host opens 98 goat doors, switching to the sole remaining unopened door wins with probability 99/100.

The result assumes the host always knows the prize location, always opens a different door containing a goat, and always offers the chance to switch. Changing those rules can change the probabilities.

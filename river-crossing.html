The Mathematics Behind This Puzzle

River crossing puzzles are examples of state-space problems. A state records who is on each bank and where the boat is. A legal move changes one state into another without breaking the safety rules.

Finding the shortest solution

We can represent the puzzle as a graph: each safe state is a vertex and each legal crossing is an edge. Breadth-first search explores states in increasing order of distance from the start, so the first route it finds to the goal uses the fewest crossings.

A mathematical challenge

Why does the farmer, wolf, goat and cabbage puzzle require at least seven crossings?

Show Explanation

The goat must cross first, because leaving the wolf with the goat or the goat with the cabbage is unsafe. The farmer must then return alone. To transport either the wolf or cabbage, the farmer must bring the goat back on the next return trip. After delivering the other item, the farmer returns alone and finally takes the goat across. This requires four outward crossings and three returns: seven crossings.

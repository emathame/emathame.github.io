Tower of Hanoi

The Mathematics Behind This Puzzle

The Tower of Hanoi is a classic example of recursion: solving a large problem by first solving a smaller version of the same problem.

Why is the minimum number of moves 2n − 1?

To move a tower of n discs, first move the top n − 1 discs to the spare peg. Move the largest disc to its destination, then move the n − 1 discs onto it.

If T(n) is the minimum number of moves, then T(n) = 2T(n − 1) + 1, with T(1) = 1. This gives T(n) = 2n − 1.

A mathematical challenge

Suppose a person makes exactly one move every second, without stopping. How long would it take to complete the optimal solution for a tower of 30 discs?

Show Explanation

The minimum is 230 − 1 = 1,073,741,823 moves. At one move per second, this is approximately 34 years, without breaks!

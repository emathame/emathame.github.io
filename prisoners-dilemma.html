The Mathematics Behind the Prisoner's Dilemma

In the one-round Prisoner's Dilemma, Betray is the individually rational choice regardless of the other player's decision: it gives 5 instead of 3 if the other cooperates, and 1 instead of 0 if the other betrays. Therefore, (Betray, Betray) is a Nash equilibrium, even though both players would earn more by cooperating.

In repeated games, players can respond to previous behaviour. Strategies such as Tit for Tat cooperate initially and then copy the other player's previous choice. This can reward cooperation while discouraging betrayal. However, no single strategy guarantees the highest score against every possible opponent.

Why does the computer sometimes betray?

The computer's choices depend on the difficulty level. Easy uses a random strategy, Standard uses Tit for Tat, and Challenge uses a more demanding strategy that retaliates after betrayal and occasionally forgives. The game does not read your current choice before selecting its move.

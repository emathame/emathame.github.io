The Mathematics Behind the Chaos Game

Although each vertex is chosen randomly, the resulting points are not distributed randomly throughout the triangle. Instead, they accumulate on the Sierpiński triangle, a self-similar fractal.

Why does the pattern emerge?

Each move maps the current point halfway towards one of three vertices. These three transformations shrink distances by a factor of 1/2. Together, they repeatedly place points into three smaller corner triangles, leaving the central inverted triangle empty in the limiting pattern. The same process repeats at every scale.

A mathematical challenge

Suppose a Sierpiński triangle is made by keeping three copies of itself, each scaled to half the original side length. What is its fractal dimension?

Show Explanation

If a self-similar figure consists of N copies scaled by a factor r, its similarity dimension d satisfies N × rd = 1.

Here, 3 × (1/2)d = 1, so 2d = 3 and d = log(3) / log(2) ≈ 1.585. Its dimension lies between that of a line and a filled-in region.

Does the starting point matter?

For any finite starting point, repeated iterations approach the same fractal pattern. The first few points can lie outside the final pattern, but their influence fades as more points are plotted.

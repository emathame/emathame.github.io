The law of large numbers

As the number of independent fair coin tosses grows, the proportion of heads tends to approach 50%. This does not mean the coin must compensate for an earlier imbalance. For example, after 100 heads and 80 tails, the next toss still has a 50% probability of heads.

Why is this called the gambler's fallacy?

The fallacy is the belief that a random outcome becomes more or less likely simply because it has appeared frequently or rarely in recent independent trials. A run of heads can be unusual, but once it has occurred, it does not alter the probability of the next toss.

Important: These results assume a fair coin and independent tosses. A biased coin or a process with dependent outcomes would require a different model.

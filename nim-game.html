The Mathematics Behind This Puzzle

Nim is an example of an impartial combinatorial game: both players have the same possible moves, and there is no chance involved. In this version, taking the last object wins (called normal play).

What is the nim-sum?

Write each pile size in binary, then combine the numbers using exclusive OR (XOR). In each binary column, the result is 1 if an odd number of piles have a 1 in that column, and 0 otherwise.

For example, piles of 3, 4 and 5 have binary forms 011, 100 and 101. Their nim-sum is 011 ⊕ 100 ⊕ 101 = 010, or 2.

Why does the nim-sum matter?

With perfect play, a position with nim-sum 0 is losing for the player whose turn it is, provided the opponent continues to play correctly. From a position with a nonzero nim-sum, there is always a legal move that makes it zero.

After every opponent move, an expert player aims to restore a zero nim-sum. The computer uses this strategy at Challenge level.

A mathematical challenge

You are facing piles of 3, 4 and 5 objects. Which pile should you change, and how many objects should you remove, to leave a nim-sum of zero?

Show Explanation

The nim-sum is 3 ⊕ 4 ⊕ 5 = 2. We want to change one pile so that the new nim-sum is zero.

For the pile of 3, calculate 3 ⊕ 2 = 1. Reducing that pile from 3 to 1 removes 2 objects.

The remaining piles are 1, 4 and 5, and 1 ⊕ 4 ⊕ 5 = 0. This is a winning move against an opponent who cannot force a win from a zero nim-sum.

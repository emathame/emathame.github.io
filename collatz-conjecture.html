The mathematics

1. How do the rules work?

Starting at 6: 6 → 3 → 10 → 5 → 16 → 8 → 4 → 2 → 1. Even numbers are halved; odd numbers become 3n + 1. This example takes 8 steps.

2. What is the Collatz conjecture?

The conjecture states that every positive whole number eventually reaches 1 when these rules are repeated. After reaching 1, the rules produce the repeating cycle 1 → 4 → 2 → 1. The conjecture has been tested for vast ranges of starting numbers, but no proof covering every positive integer is known.

3. Why can the numbers get so large?

An odd step changes n into 3n + 1, which is larger than n. Later even steps divide the number by 2. These rises and falls can produce unexpectedly high peaks. For example, the sequence starting at 27 climbs to 9,232 before eventually reaching 1.

4. Does testing many numbers prove the conjecture?

No. Even if a computer verifies trillions of cases, there are infinitely many positive integers. A proof would need to establish the claim for all of them, not just those checked.

5. What is stopping time?

In this activity, total steps to 1 means the number of rule applications needed to first reach 1. Some mathematicians use “stopping time” for the first time the sequence drops below its starting value, so the terminology is not always identical.
